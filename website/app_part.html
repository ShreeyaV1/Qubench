A fair jury needs a fair algorithm

Many different juries can be equally balanced. A fair system must pick each of them with the same chance. This test uses all 20,000 Qiskit measurements of each method.

Running on quantum hardware

Honest finding: the fair circuit is about 10× deeper than the standard one. On today's noisy chips that noise washes out its fairness advantage. The algorithm is provably fair; the hardware is not yet good enough to run it faithfully.
How it works

A real fairness problem goes in, a quantum computer does the work, and a jury plus a checkable receipt comes out.

Step 1

Bridge in

Each potential juror becomes one qubit (1 = selected). A cost scores how far a jury's mix of neighbourhood, age and gender is from the community's, and becomes a quantum operator.

Step 2

Quantum

Standard QAOA finds balanced juries but plays favourites. Fair QAOA starts from every jury of the right size at once and uses a Grover mixer, which guarantees equally fair juries get equal chances.

Step 3

Bridge out

The circuit is measured thousands of times. One of the fairest measured juries is picked at random, keeping the algorithm's own odds, and a receipt records every setting so the draw can be checked.

Honest limits
  • Small pools only: each juror is one qubit, and a normal computer solves these sizes easily.
  • Fair juries don't yet mean equal chances for every individual person.
  • Today's hardware is too noisy to run the fair circuit faithfully.
  • Synthetic people only; we balance neighbourhood, age group and gender, not race directly.
